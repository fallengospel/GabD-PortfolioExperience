How I got here

Five stops, from a province in Samar to a product team in Cebu. Each one changed the question I ask first.

  1. 2014–2019 Calbayog

    Learning to read a map

    BS Computer Science, Northwest Samar State University · GIS mapping intern

    I studied Computer Science in Calbayog City. My thesis was a mobile app that used the Elo rating system, the one used to rank chess players, to show what each student was strong in.

    During my internship with the Bureau of Internal Revenue I mapped barangays in Manifold GIS, one establishment and one boundary at a time. It was slow, careful work. It taught me that a map only matters if someone else can read it.

    Still the best lesson I've had about documentation.

  2. 2021–2023 Calbayog, hybrid

    Building what was asked

    Junior Front-End Developer, DX Form PH · Freelance album-cover designer, Fiverr

    My first job at DX Form was front-end development: web and mobile apps, built by an agile team. I loved the moment a screen finally worked.

    I also noticed how often a screen worked perfectly and still confused the person using it. Designing album covers for musicians on the side taught me the same thing from another angle: listen for what people mean, not only what they say.

    The bug I remember most wasn't in the code. It was in the brief.

  3. 2023–2024 On-site

    Keeping people moving

    Junior Project Manager, DX Form PH

    Then I moved from writing the code to looking after the people who write it. My progress started to show up in other people's work instead of my own.

    Yesterday

    Moved from developer to project manager.

    Today

    Weekly standups across web and mobile projects.
    Testing before every release.
    Running our OJT program and bootcamps for new developers.

    Blocker

    Me, at first. I had to stop fixing things myself and start making room for others to fix them.

    Watching an intern ship their first feature felt better than shipping my own.

  4. 2025–2026 Remote

    Asking who it's for

    UX/UI Designer, Talleco

    I left to work as a UX/UI designer, remotely. Research, wireframes, high-fidelity screens, design systems, developer handoff, usability sessions. For the first time, my work started before anything was built.

    I learned to sit with a problem longer, and to defend a decision with evidence instead of taste.

    Remote work is quiet. I learned to over-communicate, kindly.

  5. 2026–now Cebu

    Bringing it together

    Project / Product Manager, DX Form PH

    In May 2026 I came back to DX Form as a Project and Product Manager, and as its first in-house UX/UI designer. I lead the teams, I'm the client's main contact, and I design the products.

    I use AI to explore faster and test more ideas than I could alone. The decisions are still mine to make, and to explain. The work below is what that looks like.

    Now

    • Lead delivery and design for client products
    • Grow the DX Design System

    Next

    • Every DX product built from one system

    Later

    • Write down what I've learned, for the next first designer

    Coming back felt like returning to a house I'd helped build, and finally knowing where the light switches should go.
